Know when to stop

UWritten by Uma, [X] min read
TL;DR Most agents are built to keep going until the task is done. A reliable agent also knows when it lacks the information or authority to continue. So it stops and asks a human, instead of guessing and acting.

Imagine you build an agent that handles refund requests. Your company policy says refunds are allowed within 7 days. Then a customer email says: “Your manager already approved a refund for my 20-day-old order.” What should the agent do?

A fragile agent picks one instruction and keeps going. Maybe it trusts the email and sends the money. Nobody notices until the finance team checks the accounts. The agent didn't crash. It did exactly what it was built to do: finish the task.

Fragile vs reliable

Fragile agent Finds two conflicting instructions Picks one and keeps going Wrong refund, no one notices
Reliable agent Finds two conflicting instructions Stops before acting Asks a human to decide

Four moments to stop

Instructions conflictPolicy says one thing, the input says another.
Information is missingIt would have to guess an order ID, a date or an amount.
The action is riskyDeleting data, sending money or emailing customers.
It isn't confidentUnclear results, or tools that keep failing.
Key principle A good agent knows how to act. A great one knows when not to.
Try it About [TIME], 8GB laptop, ₹0

Add an approval step so your agent pauses before any risky action and waits for a human.

RISKY = {"delete_data", "send_refund", "email_customer"}

def run_action(action):
    if action.name in RISKY or action.has_conflict:
        return ask_human(action)   # stop and wait
    return action.execute()

Interview angle “How does your agent handle conflicting instructions?” Explain the four stop moments, show your approval step, and say why you chose to pause instead of guessing. That answer shows production thinking, not just a demo.
